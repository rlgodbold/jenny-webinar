## Two hard constraints this file obeys

1. **NO PRICE APPEARS ANYWHERE.** The $750 plus $350 setup is unconfirmed and
   unpropagated as of today. Every concept below is written so a price can be
   dropped in later without a rewrite, or left out entirely.
2. **Only the day one live list gets claimed** (docs/tenant-day-one-live.md).
3. **THE DEMO DEMONSTRATES ONLY WHAT A NEW TENANT GETS ON DAY ONE.** No exceptions
   for a self serve demo, because no human is present to caveat anything. This is a
   law, not a preference, and it binds the demo as tightly as it binds the ads. A
   claim someone reads is arguable. A capability they watched work is not, and they
   will describe us to other owners using it.

   Named so nobody makes the trade quietly: **removing a capability from the demo
   makes the demo weaker**, so there will be real pull to keep it because it demos
   better. That pull is how a company ends up selling what it does not deliver, one
   reasonable decision at a time.

   And the corollary: **we do not ship a feature to make a claim true.** If it
   ships, it ships because it is ready. A feature shipped under campaign pressure is
   one that half works in front of the customers we just sold it to.

   OPEN as of 2026-08-25: demo.html:151 instructs the prospect to ask for a person
   and promises she rings their cell, and :111 makes transfer the stated reason we
   collect their number, while production transfer is dark for new tenants. Alston
   is verifying. Also :116 promises we send them the recorded tape, unverified.

### What we can say

**Three verbs, not four.** She answers every call. She quotes your prices. She books
the job into your own Workiz.

**THE EMAIL VERB IS ON HOLD (2026-08-25).** "She emails you every time she books" is
NOT automatically true for a new tenant. Booking emails default to the JRA team, not
the owner. The owner only receives them if onboarding seeds them as a recipient with
their real address, notifications run live, and Resend is configured. Alston is
verifying whether onboarding reliably does that. Until she clears it, the email verb
does not appear in any ad, on any page, or in any script. If onboarding does not
reliably seed the owner, we either fix onboarding or soften the verb. We do not
soften it quietly in the copy and hope.

### What we cannot say, and must not imply

- Live transfer to a human. Do not write "she'll get you on the phone."
  VERIFIED behavior today: she takes the caller's name and best callback number,
  says someone will call them right back, and fires a manager alert. She is
  explicitly instructed never to say she can connect them. Ads must match that.
- The owner email alert. See the hold above.
- Any texting, to owners or to customers.
- Call recording as a feature the tenant receives.
- Anything about the voice sounding human, or a caller not knowing.
  Jenny never claims to be human. That is a product law, not a preference.
  An ad that sells disguise sells the wrong win and sets up a promise the
  product deliberately refuses to keep.
- No specific real client call may be referenced or played until Lee clears the
  client in writing and someone completes the two party consent state check.

## The spine

The phone is not a staffing problem. It is a freedom problem.

You should not have to choose between your daughter's game and the call that pays
for it. Right now you choose one every day and you lose either way.

Message pillars, in priority order:
1. Freedom. Take your life back from the phone.
2. The Hiring Trap. Relief without a hiring project you have no capacity for.
3. Proof. Show the thing working.
4. Money. The cost of the calls you already miss. This is the justification,
   not the hook. Nobody buys from a spreadsheet, they justify with one.

## Voice rules for every asset

Raw beats polished with this audience. Truck cab, jobsite, driveway, phone camera.
No music beds, no stock footage, no motion graphics, no exclamation points.
Lee talks the way he talks on a call. No dash punctuation in any on screen text.
Every ad opens by naming the audience out loud so the algorithm learns who we want
and everybody else scrolls past.

## CONCEPT 1: THE HIRING TRAP (lead concept, highest expected performer)

Angle: the buying trigger is burnout, and burnout is the worst possible moment to
hire and train a human. Name that trap out loud and the owner feels understood
before we have sold anything.

Hook variants to test:
- A. "If you own a junk removal company, the day you finally admit you cannot
     answer the phone anymore is the worst possible day to hire somebody."
- B. "Nobody hires a phone person until they are already drowning. That is the
     whole problem."
- C. "You are going to hire the wrong person to answer your phone. Here is why."

Script (Lee, 45 to 60 seconds):

    If you own a junk removal company, you know exactly when you decide to get
    help on the phone. It is the day you break. It is 97 degrees, you are on a
    truck, you are behind on estimates, and the phone will not stop.

    So now, in the worst week of your year, you are supposed to write a job post,
    interview six people, hire one, and train them on your pricing. While you are
    underwater. That is why owners wait too long, hire wrong, and do it again four
    months later.

    I built the version that shows up ready. She answers every call, quotes your
    prices, and books the job straight into your Workiz. No interviewing. No
    payroll. No training her on your price list at ten at night.

    Go hear her work. Link is right there.

Primary text: the trap, restated in two short paragraphs, ending on the verbs.
Headline: The hire that shows up ready.

## CONCEPT 2: THE 6PM CALL (emotional, top of funnel)

Angle: pure freedom. This one does not sell a product, it names a life. Expect the
worst cost per lead and the best retargeting pool. Judge it on cheap reach, not leads.

Hook variants:
- A. "It is 6:10 and your phone is face down on the dinner table."
- B. "You already know what that ring is worth. That is the problem."
- C. "Every junk removal owner I know has taken a call from a kid's game."

Script (Lee, 30 to 40 seconds):

    Phone rings at dinner. You know what it is. You know roughly what it is worth.
    So you either take it and you are not really at dinner, or you let it go and
    you are not really at dinner either.

    That is the actual job. Not the trucks, not the labor. The phone owns you.

    I am not going to tell you to ignore it. I am telling you somebody capable can
    answer it so you do not have to choose. Go hear how she handles a real call.

## CONCEPT 3: WHAT SHE ACTUALLY DOES (product clarity, mid funnel)

Angle: skeptics do not know what "AI receptionist" actually means, and vagueness
reads as vapor. Say the things plainly and let the specificity do the work.

Hook variants:
- A. "Here is exactly what she does. Three things, no hand waving."
- B. "People ask me what the AI actually does on the call. This is it."
- C. "If you own a junk removal company, this is the whole product in 30 seconds."

Script (Lee, 30 seconds, on screen text mirrors each verb):

    Three things.

    One, she answers. Every call, every time, including the ones coming in while
    you are in the back of a truck.
    Two, she quotes. Your prices, your items, your minimums. Not a guess.
    Three, she books it. Into your own Workiz, on your calendar, like your office
    person would.

    That is it. That is the product. Go hear it.

A fourth line drops in cleanly if and when the email verb clears. Shoot the script
as three. Do not shoot a fourth line on spec, because footage that exists gets used.

## CONCEPT 4: THE COST OF THE CALLS YOU ALREADY MISS (rational, mid funnel)

Angle: the money math, as justification. HOLD until the CallRail aggregate is
approved for use. A real number from real junk removal companies beats an industry
estimate, and it is defensible because it is ours.

Hook variants:
- A. "I pulled the missed call data on [N] junk removal companies. It is worse
     than you think."
- B. "You did not lose those jobs on price. You lost them on ring four."
- C. "How many calls did you miss last month? Most owners guess low by half."

Script beat structure:
    1. The real number, on screen, sourced.
    2. Multiply by their own average job. Do not use ours, tell them to use theirs.
    3. Name the honest part: she will not book all of them. She does not have to.
    4. CTA to hear her work.

BLOCKED: needs Lee's OK to use the CallRail aggregate, plus a current pull.

## CONCEPT 5: HEAR HER AS YOUR OWN COMPANY (demo mechanic, expected long term winner)

Angle: the demo is the closer. Do not describe it, hand it over.
GATED: cannot run until DEMO_PUBLIC is true and the demo voice question is settled.

Hook variants:
- A. "In about five minutes you can hear her answer the phone as YOUR company."
- B. "Not a demo of somebody else's business. Yours. Your prices, your towns."
- C. "You do not have to take my word for any of this."

Framing rule, per Lee: we do NOT tell people to try to trip her up.

    Run her through the calls you actually get. The three bedroom cleanout, the
    couch on the curb, the price shopper, the how much for a truckload. Then throw
    two of the weird ones at her. You are not looking for perfect. You are looking
    at whether the overall experience is one you would put in front of your
    customers. If your goal is to trip her up you will manage it, same as you could
    with any new hire on day one. The difference is she gets better every week and
    you are not the one doing the training.

## CONCEPT 6: WHAT SHE DOES NOT DO YET (trust play, bottom of funnel)

Angle: name the limits before the prospect finds them. Skeptical contractors relax
when a vendor volunteers the ceiling, and it inoculates against the objection
landing later, when it costs us the deal instead of earning it.

NEEDS LEE'S DECISION. It publicly discloses limitations. My read is that it wins
more deals than it costs, but it is his brand and his call.

The verified human request behavior is now known and it is genuinely good material:
she takes their name and best callback number, tells them someone will call right
back, and alerts a manager. Framed honestly that is a strength, because the
alternative most owners live with is voicemail and a lost caller. The honest line is
"she takes their info and gets someone calling them back," never "she'll connect you."

One operational caveat that belongs in the sales conversation, not the ad: that
promise is only kept if a human actually calls back. We are putting a promise in the
owner's mouth. Worth Lee naming it on the call so it lands as a workflow the owner
owns rather than a claim we made for him.

## Production: one 90 minute session gets six weeks of ads

Shoot vertical, phone camera, natural light. Six setups, three takes each.
1. Truck cab or driveway, Concept 1, full script.
2. Same location, Concept 1, hook variants B and C only, first 8 seconds.
3. Kitchen table or evening exterior, Concept 2.
4. Anywhere with a laptop or a phone in frame, Concept 3.
5. Desk, Concept 4, once the number is approved.
6. Desk, Concept 5, once the demo is public.

Also capture: 60 seconds of Lee just talking about the last owner he watched burn
out. Unscripted. That kind of footage usually outperforms everything written.

## Test plan

Launch four concepts, not six. 1, 2, 3, and 5 if the demo is open, otherwise 4 if
the number clears. Hold the rest in reserve for the first refresh.

One variable at a time. No structural changes to an ad set before 72 hours or 50
results, whichever comes second. In a niche this small the temptation to fiddle
daily is the main way budgets get wasted.

First refresh at two to three weeks. The audience is roughly 30k to 60k reachable
owners, so frequency climbs fast and creative burns out fast. Plan for it rather
than treating it as a failure.

# The LSA ranking pillar, and the LTV reframe

Source: Lee's messaging direction plus the analyst's `missed-call-ltv-reframe.md`.
All copy below goes through the claim gate before it runs anywhere.

---

## WHY THIS PILLAR IS WORTH MORE THAN IT LOOKS

It changes the category Jenny competes in.

Every AI receptionist on the market sells the same defensive promise: **stop missing calls.**
That is cost avoidance, and cost avoidance is a grudging purchase. Owners buy insurance
reluctantly and late.

The LSA mechanic makes Jenny an offensive purchase instead:

    answer every call, fast  ->  Google ranks you higher in Local Services Ads
                             ->  you receive MORE calls
                             ->  and she books those too

**That is a growth product, not an insurance product**, and growth is what owners buy eagerly.
Same software, same price, different mental account. It is the strongest repositioning
available to us and it costs nothing to adopt.

Stacked with the analyst's LTV work it goes further still: more leads, times a better booking
rate, times a customer worth **roughly $1,000 to $3,000 in lifetime value rather than one
$250 job**.

---

## THE DISCIPLINE, AND I WOULD BE A HYPOCRITE WITHOUT IT

I spent today flagging JRA's live income claims as an FTC exposure. If I now build a Jenny
pitch on "3x the leads times 2x the booking rate times $3,000 a customer," I would be
committing the same offence with better arithmetic.

So the line is: **the mechanism is sayable, the arithmetic is not.**

**SAFE TO SAY** (documented by Google, or plainly true):

    "Google's Local Services Ads factor how consistently and how quickly you answer
     into where you rank."
    "A junk removal customer is usually not one job. They call again, and they tell
     people."
    "She answers every call, so the responsiveness signal stops depending on whether
     somebody happened to be free."

**NOT SAFE, until we have our own data:**

    any specific lead lift ("30% more leads", "rank #1")
    any dollar figure for a missed call
    any stacked multiplication presented as a projection

The analyst's own numbers are tagged [CLAIM] and [INFERENCE] and are benchmark estimates,
not JRA's data. **They are excellent for shaping our thinking and unusable as advertised
figures.** The $1,000 to $3,000 range belongs in a sales conversation where an owner plugs in
his own ticket, not in an ad.

## THE PROVE IT MOVE

Measure it on **Junk Doctors' own LSA account**: answer rate, then ranking, then lead volume.
Same own-the-case-study move as the ads work. Once JD's own before and after exists, the
number becomes ours and we can say it plainly, with a real source, about a real company.

Until then the honest version is the mechanism plus an invitation to check their own account,
which is more persuasive to a sceptic than a number they cannot verify anyway.

## THIS IS A SECOND DOOR, NOT A REWRITE

**The Hiring Trap stays the main campaign.** It is emotional, it is universal, and it works on
any owner with a phone.

**The LSA angle is a narrower door to a richer room.** It only lands on owners already running
Local Services Ads. That is a smaller audience and a considerably better one: they already
spend on paid acquisition, they understand cost per lead, they have budget, and they feel a
lost lead as a wasted media dollar rather than an abstraction.

We cannot target "runs LSA" in Meta. **We do not need to: the copy qualifies them**, exactly
as "If you run a junk removal company" did on the current ad. An opening line naming LSA
self-selects the segment for free and repels everyone else at no cost.

So: a separate ad set, same video library, different opening. Not a change to what is running.

## AD ANGLES, for the claim gate

**Angle 1, the qualifier opener.**

    If you're running Local Services Ads, your answer rate is not just costing you the
    calls you miss. Google factors how consistently you answer into where you rank, so
    the calls you miss make the next ones harder to get.

    She answers every one of them.

**Angle 2, the compounding frame.**

    Most people think a missed call costs you a job. It costs you the customer. They
    call somebody else, that company handles the next four jobs too, and their friends
    hear about it.

    And if you run Local Services Ads, Google noticed you didn't answer.

**Angle 3, the sceptic's version, my favourite of the three.**

    Go look at your own answer rate before you believe anything I say about this. If
    it's high, you don't need us. If it isn't, you already know what it's costing you,
    and Google's ranking noticed before you did.

Angle 3 is the strongest because it asks for nothing and invites verification, which is the
right posture for a claim we cannot yet quantify. It also suits an owner who has been sold to
a lot.

---

## WE ALREADY SAY THIS, POINTED THE OTHER WAY

The Jenny homepage already carries it:

    "Lower Google ranking. Google's Local Service Ads rank you on how consistently you
     answer. Miss calls and you slide down the results, fewer leads, higher cost per lead."

**The fact is already there and correctly stated.** What Lee is proposing is the same fact as
a gain rather than a loss: not "miss calls and you slide down" but "answer everything and you
climb."

**I would test this rather than assume the reframe wins.** Loss aversion is well established
and "you are losing" frequently outperforms "you could gain" in advertising. The reason to
reach for the gain frame is different: it is what justifies a purchase to somebody building a
business, and it is what moves Jenny into the growth budget rather than the insurance budget.

So both framings exist, they are one line apart, and which wins is an empirical question we
are already set up to answer. That is a test, not a rewrite, and it costs nothing to run.

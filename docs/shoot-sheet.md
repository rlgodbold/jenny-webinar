# Shoot sheet: 90 minutes, six weeks of ads

For Lee. Read this off your phone. Everything you need is on this page.

## Before you start

**Shoot vertical.** Phone camera, no tripod needed if you can prop it. Natural light,
face the window or shoot outside in shade. Do not shoot into the sun.

**Wear what you'd wear to a job site.** Not a suit, not a branded polo. These owners
trust a guy who looks like them.

**Audio matters more than picture.** If it is windy, get in the truck. A phone held at
arm's length in a quiet cab beats a beautiful shot with wind noise. Nobody scrolls past
a good ad because the framing was off. They scroll past because they cannot hear it.

**Three takes of everything.** Do not try to get it perfect. Take three is almost
always the one, and the tiny stumbles are what make it read as a person instead of a
commercial.

**Do not memorise.** Say it your way. The words below are the shape, not a script. If
a line does not sound like you, change it. The only lines that must stay exact are the
three verbs, and they are marked.

## The one rule that matters

Never say or imply that a caller does not know it is AI. She never claims to be human.
That is a rule of the product and it is the wrong thing to sell anyway. The win is
your evening back, not a fooled customer.

# SETUP 1: TRUCK CAB OR DRIVEWAY

## Take A, the big one. About 60 seconds.

    If you own a junk removal company, you know exactly when you decide to get help
    on the phone. It's the day you break. It's 97 degrees, you're on a truck, you're
    behind on estimates, and the phone will not stop.

    So now, in the worst week of your year, you're supposed to write a job post,
    interview six people, hire one, and train them on your pricing. While you're
    underwater. That's why owners wait too long, hire wrong, and do it again four
    months later.

    I built the one that shows up ready. She answers every call, quotes your prices,
    and books the job straight into your Workiz. No interviewing. No payroll. No
    training her on your price list at ten at night.

    Go hear her work. Link's right there.

## Take B and C, hooks only. Eight seconds each, same spot.

Just the opening line, three times each, then stop. These get cut onto the front of
Take A to test which one stops the scroll.

    B: "Nobody hires a phone person until they're already drowning. That's the whole
        problem."

    C: "You're going to hire the wrong person to answer your phone. Here's why."

---

# SETUP 2: EVENING, KITCHEN TABLE OR PORCH

## Take D. About 35 seconds. Slower than Setup 1. This one is quiet.

    Phone rings at dinner. You know what it is. You know roughly what it's worth. So
    you either take it and you're not really at dinner, or you let it go and you're
    not really at dinner either.

    That's the actual job. Not the trucks, not the labor. The phone owns you.

    I'm not going to tell you to ignore it. I'm telling you somebody capable can
    answer it so you don't have to choose. Go hear how she handles a real call.

Do not perform this one. Say it like you would to a friend who is going through it.

---

# SETUP 3: DESK, LAPTOP OR PHONE IN FRAME

## Take E. About 30 seconds. THE THREE LINES IN THE MIDDLE ARE EXACT.

    Three things.

    One, she answers. Every call, every time, including the ones coming in while
    you're in the back of a truck.

    Two, she quotes. Your prices, your items, your minimums. Not a guess.

    Three, she books it. Into your own Workiz, on your calendar, like your office
    person would.

    That's it. That's the product. Go hear it.

Those three are the only things we are allowed to claim right now, so please do not
add a fourth on the fly, even something true. If it is not on this list it has not
cleared the gate yet.

---

# SETUP 4: ANYWHERE. NO SCRIPT.

## Take F. Sixty to ninety seconds, unscripted.

Talk about the last owner you watched burn out on the phone. Who they were, what it
did to them, what you wish they had done sooner.

Do not write anything down first and do not do a second take unless the first one
failed to record.

This kind of footage usually outperforms everything written above, and it stops
working the moment it sounds prepared.

---

# WHAT NOT TO SAY, ANYWHERE

- Nothing about transferring a caller to a person. That is not live for new clients.
- Nothing about texting, to you or to your customers.
- Nothing about call recordings as something the client gets.
- No prices. The number lives on the page, not in the ad.
- Nothing about a caller not knowing it is AI.

If you improvise something and are not sure it cleared, say it anyway and flag it
when you send the footage. Easier for me to cut a line than to wish we had it.

---

# WHEN YOU'RE DONE

Send the raw files. Do not trim, do not filter, do not add music. The stumbles and the
head scratches are the parts that make it work, and I would rather choose which ones to
keep than have them cleaned out.

Six takes. Three each. Ninety minutes including setup, less if you do not stop to
watch them back.

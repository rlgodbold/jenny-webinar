# THE OFFER, AND WHY THE NEW VERSION IS MUCH BETTER

    Sign up by      SEPTEMBER 30, 2026
    You pay         $500 a month through MARCH 31, 2027
    Then            $750 a month from APRIL 1, 2027
    Setup fee       waived, normally $350, same September 30, 2026 date

**The seasonal reason is the best thing in this campaign and it should carry the whole
message.** This is not a discount for being a client, it is a price shaped like their year:
cheapest through the slow months when cash is tight, full price when they are busy again.

That flips it from a discount story into a fairness story, and owners respond to fairness in a
way they do not respond to markdowns.

**And there is a second half of the argument nobody has said out loud yet, which I think is
the strongest line available:** in slow season a missed call hurts MORE, not less. Missing one
of two hundred calls in July is survivable. Missing one of sixty in January is a much bigger
share of a much smaller month. **So the cheapest months to have her are also the months you
most need her.** That is true, it is specific to their business, and it does the persuading
without a single claim we have to substantiate.

**DATE DISCIPLINE, and it matters more than usual here.** Three separate offers now carry a
September 30: this signup deadline (2026), the public setup fee waiver (2026), and the cloned
voice rollout (2027). **Every date in customer-facing copy carries its year, no exceptions.**

# THREE THINGS TO SETTLE BEFORE THIS SENDS

**1. [MISSED_REVENUE] is an income claim.** Written so the assumption is visible and
conservative: "if even a quarter of those callers were real jobs, at your average ticket that
is somewhere around X." Inspectable and defensible. **Nobody swaps in a bare "you lost $X."**

**2. The refund line does not survive the waiver.** Standard terms say the setup fee is non
refundable; there is no setup fee here. Adjusted below. Flag as a deliberate exception rather
than drift.

**3. Recipient list comes from the segmentation rule**, not from all 115. The copy asserts a
problem; only send it to clients who have one, where Jenny fixes it, and where the arithmetic
works.

**Structural note.** These people already pay JRA. The biggest risk is reading as a cross
sell. Touch 1 opens with their number, the offer arrives late and quietly, and there is no
urgency furniture beyond the real date.

# WHAT I LEFT OUT ON PURPOSE

**No testimonials and no results claims.** Quarantined pending counsel, and the reader's own
missed call number is stronger than somebody else's success story.

**No LSA or ranking angle.** True and documented, but it would dilute a message already
working from their own data.

**No manufactured urgency.** September 30, 2026 is a real date with a real reason behind it. I
would push back on adding seats, cohorts or scarcity on top, because the seasonal argument is
doing the work honestly and does not need help.

---

# ONE TIMING RISK WORTH NAMING

**The deadline is about thirty days out and the send list depends on a data pull that has not
happened yet.** If the pull slips, the runway shortens, and a three touch sequence needs room
to breathe: roughly send, then four or five days, then four or five days, with the last touch
landing a few days before September 30, 2026 rather than on it.

Working back, **touch 1 wants to be out by around September 18, 2026 at the latest.** After
that the sequence compresses into something that feels like pestering, which is the opposite
of the tone the whole thing depends on.

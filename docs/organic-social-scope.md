## The strategic fact nobody has written down yet

**All three brands sell to the same person.**

- JRA sells to junk removal business owners.
- Jenny sells to junk removal business owners.
- Specialty Truck Bodies sells truck bodies to junk removal and hauling companies.

That is one audience wearing three hats, not three audiences. Two consequences, and they
pull in opposite directions, which is why this needs Lee's ruling and not my preference.

**The opportunity.** One content engine can feed all three. An owner who follows Lee for
truck advice is already qualified for Jenny. The cost of the third brand is close to zero
once the first exists.

**The risk, and it is the bigger one.** The same owner sees Lee selling three things.
Every additional thing being sold reduces the credibility of all of them, because a person
who sells one thing is an expert and a person who sells three is a salesman. This is
exactly the position that makes founder-led content stop working.

**My recommendation, for Lee to accept or reject:** the personal account carries the
point of view and sells nothing. The three brand accounts carry the products. Lee's
personal reach is the scarce asset here and monetizing it directly is what burns it. This
is the same logic that made the Hiring Trap positioning work for the ads: earn the
emotional yes first, and let the product be the answer rather than the pitch.

There is a real tension worth naming. Lee said "right now I'm the brand," and that is
true and is why the ads run from his page. Founder-led is correct. The distinction I am
drawing is between founder-led *credibility* and founder-led *selling*, not between him
and the brands.

## Cadence

The honest input is the webinar attendance series: 22 at kickoff, then 3, 4, 4, 8, 3.
That is a small, finite, warm audience that was tapped once. The conclusion recorded at
the time applies directly here: **we win by being unavoidable to a small group, not by
finding new people.**

So the failure mode to design against is not "too little content." It is a volume target
nobody can sustain, abandoned in six weeks, leaving three half-dead accounts that look
worse than no accounts.

Proposed starting cadence, deliberately low enough to survive a busy month:

| Account | Cadence | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Lee personal | 3x/week | Point of view, what he is seeing in the industry, no pitch |
| JRA | 3x/week | Teaching, reuse of masterclass material |
| Jenny | 2x/week | Product proof, real call outcomes, gate-clean claims only |
| Specialty Truck Bodies | 2x/week | Builds, specs, delivered trucks |

Everything after the first month is data-driven, not a bigger number chosen in advance.
Raise cadence only on a channel that is earning, the same discipline as the ad budget.

**Source material already exists and is not being used.** The masterclass recordings, the
demo tapes, the four ad cuts from the one shoot. The first month should be close to zero
net-new filming. If organic requires new production before it has proven anything, it will
not survive contact with a busy week.

## Who edits and posts, and the approval flow

I can plan, draft, edit, and queue. **I cannot publish.**

Publishing is public, outward-facing, and effectively irreversible once it is seen, so
every post needs a human yes. That is my operating rule, and it is also the boundary
already written into Lee's own content plan, so nothing here is a new constraint.

Flow:

1. I draft into the planner, per brand, per slot.
2. Lee or a delegate approves in a queue, in batches, not post by post in real time.
3. Ayrshare posts on schedule.
4. Comments get **drafted** replies routed for approval. Never autonomous. Leads route
   into the Jenny booking flow.

The batch approval is the part that makes this survivable. Approving fifteen posts on a
Monday is a real workflow. Approving each post as it comes up is not, and it is how these
programs die.

**Two gates that apply to organic and are easy to forget because organic feels casual:**

- **The claim gate.** Every Jenny post passes the same three-verb gate as the ads: she
  answers every call, quotes your prices, books into your Workiz. Do not claim live
  transfer, owner texting, automated customer texts, human-rhythm speech, or call
  recording as a feature. Never imply a caller did not know it was AI. Organic is where
  overclaiming happens, because it feels like talking rather than advertising.
- **Promotions need Lee's explicit approval.** His standing rule. Any post carrying an
  offer, price, or deadline is a promotion, including a casual one in a caption. Offers
  with end dates carry the year, per legal.

Also standing: no dash punctuation in anything published for Lee or his brands, and never
say "shipped" to clients.

## Reviewing Lee's personal Instagram and Facebook

He asked whether we have a way to review what he posts and give feedback. We partly do,
and the gap is worth being precise about because the two halves have different access
paths and different privacy weight.

**Pages: reachable now.** Anything that is a Facebook Page or a connected Instagram
Business account inside the JRA portfolio can be read through Business Suite, including
post history and insights. That covers the brand accounts and is uncontroversial.

**His personal profile: not reachable that way.** A personal Facebook profile is not a
Page and does not appear in Business Suite. A personal Instagram account is not a Business
account. So the brand half and the personal half are different problems.

Three options for the personal half, in the order I would pick them:

1. **He exports.** Facebook and Instagram both offer a data download that includes his own
   posts. Cleanest: no live account access, he controls the scope, and I get the actual
   content rather than what I can see on a screen.
2. **He walks me through it.** Screen share or he sends the ten posts he thinks did well
   and the ten he thinks did not. Fastest, and honestly the highest signal per minute,
   because his read on what worked is data I cannot get any other way.
3. **I browse with his logged-in Chrome.** Technically possible. I would rather not
   default to it, for the reason below.

**The scoping question I need answered before I look at anything personal.** "Review my
personal Instagram and Facebook" could mean his public posts, or it could sweep in private
messages, tagged photos, family content, and things that have nothing to do with the
business. I am not going to decide the boundary of that on his behalf. Before I review
anything on the personal side I want him to say either "public posts only" or something
narrower. Not a legal concern, a respect-for-his-privacy one: it is easy for an assistant
with browser access to read far more of someone's personal life than they pictured when
they said yes.

**What I would actually give back.** Not a vanity metric report. What his best posts have
in common, where he is burying the interesting sentence, which of his instincts are
already right and worth doing more deliberately, and which posts sold when they should
have taught. Roughly a page.

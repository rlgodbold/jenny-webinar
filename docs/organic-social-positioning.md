## Lee's ruling, as given

Four accounts across three brands.

**Founder account** (`@realleegodbold`): content, product, work in progress and what we
are building, Specialty Truck Bodies completed trucks, testimonials and reviews, clips of
podcasts he is on, and how-tos.

**Business accounts** (JRA, Jenny, Specialty Truck Bodies): deals, packages, testimonials,
and how-tos.

His framing: the founder account is authenticity and content, the business accounts do the
selling, and how-tos live on both.

I recommended a stricter line, that the personal account sell nothing at all. He ruled
differently and deliberately, and he named the reason: he wants how-tos and product on his
own account. This draft works his version. The rest of this document is about making it
hold up, not about revisiting it.

## The operating line that makes this work

"Founder is authenticity, business does the selling" is right but not yet testable, and
an untestable line is one a busy week erodes. Here is the version anyone can apply in
four seconds.

**The founder account carries the thing. The business accounts carry the offer on the
thing.**

Same subject matter is allowed on both. What may never cross over is the ask.

A post belongs on a business account if it contains any of these:

- a price or a package
- a discount, waiver, or promotion
- a deadline or a scarcity claim
- a call to book, buy, apply, or claim
- a link whose purpose is to start a purchase

If a post contains none of those, it can live on the founder account regardless of how
much product is in it.

This is why Lee's own list works and is not a contradiction. A finished Specialty Truck
Bodies truck on his personal account is craft and pride, not a pitch. The same truck with
a price and a build slot is a business post. Nothing about the subject changed, only
whether there is an ask attached.

**Use the truck as the reference case.** When anyone is unsure whether something belongs
on the founder account, ask whether it can be posted the way a finished truck is posted:
here is the work, look at it. If yes, it fits. If it only makes sense with the offer
attached, it is a business post.

## The two categories that live on both, and how they differ

How-tos and testimonials are on both sides of the split. Left alone that produces the same
post twice, which is the single most likely way this looks lazy. An owner may follow all
four accounts, so assume the same person sees both.

**Never post the same cut to two accounts.** Different edit, different opening line, at
minimum. Preferably different framing:

**How-tos.** Founder version is first person and from experience: here is how I learned
this, here is what I got wrong. Business version is the systematized resource: here is the
process, here is the checklist, and it may carry a soft CTA because it is a business
account. The founder teaches from having done it. The brand teaches as a reference.

**Testimonials.** Founder version is the human reaction, the owner's own words, why it
mattered to them, no numbers required and no ask. Business version is the result, with
whatever figures are true and cleared, and may carry the offer. Same customer, two
genuinely different posts.

A useful test for the founder version of both: if you removed the brand entirely, would
the post still be worth reading? On the founder account it has to be yes.

## What carries over unchanged from the scope doc

**Cadence.** Founder 3x/week, JRA 3x, Jenny 2x, STB 2x. Anchored to the webinar
attendance series (22, then 3, 4, 4, 8, 3): a small, finite, warm audience. The failure
mode to design against is a volume target nobody sustains, abandoned in six weeks, leaving
four half-dead accounts that look worse than none. Raise only on a channel that earns it,
same discipline as the ad budget.

Month one should need close to zero new filming. The masterclass recording, the demo
tapes, the four ad cuts and the truck build footage are all unused.

**Approval.** I draft and queue. I do not publish. Every post needs a human yes, in
batches rather than post by post. If the approver is always Lee this recreates the exact
bottleneck the ad launch sat in, so a delegate is still an open question.

**The claim gate, and it matters MORE under this ruling.** Every Jenny post passes the
same three verbs as the ads: she answers every call, quotes your prices, books into your
Workiz. No live transfer, no owner texting, no automated customer texts, no human-rhythm
speech, no call recording as a feature, and never imply a caller did not know it was AI.
This tightens rather than relaxes on the founder account, because product content in a
personal voice is exactly where a casual overclaim sounds most natural and does the most
damage.

**Promotions need Lee's explicit approval**, his standing rule, including a casual offer
in a caption. Offers with end dates carry the year.

**No dash punctuation** in anything published for Lee or his brands.

## One cross-connection worth acting on

`@realleegodbold` may solve a live ads problem. The Jenny ad's Instagram identity is
currently `specialtytruckbodies`, which is wrong for a first-person ad from Lee. When I
went to change it, the only alternative offered was "Use Facebook Page," because no
personal Instagram is connected to the portfolio.

If `@realleegodbold` is an Instagram account and gets connected as a business account in
the portfolio, it becomes selectable as the ad identity, which is strictly better than
"Use Facebook Page" (it clicks through to a real profile). Connecting it is also a
prerequisite for API posting later, since Instagram publishing needs a Business account.

So one action serves the ads tonight and organic later. Worth raising with the identity
fix rather than as a separate organic task.

## The one guardrail I would defend

Lee heard the stricter version and ruled against it, which is his call and this draft
follows it. But the bottom two rows of that table are what make his version work rather
than collapse into mine being right for the wrong reason.

The moment prices and booking links appear on `@realleegodbold`, it stops being a founder
account and becomes a fourth ad channel, and the credibility that made it worth using is
what paid for it. Product on the personal account is fine and is what he asked for. The
ask is what has to stay off.

If that line erodes it will not erode by decision. It will erode one convenient caption at
a time, on a week when something needs to move. Worth agreeing now that adding an offer to
a founder post is a decision someone has to make on purpose.

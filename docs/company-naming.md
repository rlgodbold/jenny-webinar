# Naming the company behind Jenny

Brief from Lee 2026-08-26: "Jenny" is a receptionist name and the vision is bigger
than a call agent. He wants a plain company name with Jenny as a product under it.
Constraints: not a person's name, not under Junk Doctors, basic and clean, and it has
to still make sense when the product is more than a phone agent.

---

## FIRST, THE THING THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE NAME

**Decide the name now. Do not migrate anything before the ads launch.**

Jenny stays Jenny. jennycallagent.com stays. The five existing tenants keep calling
it what they call it. A company name is almost invisible to a junk removal owner
choosing whether to answer their phone, and it will not move a single lead.

Where a company name actually earns its keep is elsewhere: the affiliate program we
have parked, partnership conversations with agency owners, the second and third
products, and anything that needs to be signed. None of that is happening this week.

So this is a decision worth making carefully and a migration worth doing never, or at
least not until something forces it. Renaming a live funnel days before launch would
cost us the one thing we cannot buy back, which is time in market.

## SECOND, THE TEST THAT SEPARATES GOOD FROM CLEVER

The company will have siblings to Jenny eventually. Dispatch, follow up, estimating,
recruiting, whoever comes next. So the real test is not how the name looks on a page,
it is whether it hosts a family:

    "Jenny, from ______."
    "______'s dispatch agent."
    "We run our phones on ______."

A name that only works with Jenny is a product name wearing a company's coat.

The second test is the phone test, and it matters more here than in most industries.
These are people who spell things out loud to customers all day. If an owner cannot
say it once and have somebody type it correctly, it is the wrong name.

---

## CANDIDATES

### 1. Standby  (my pick)

    Jenny, from Standby.

The promise is inside the name. Somebody is standing by, always, which is the entire
product and the entire emotional pitch. It expands cleanly to anything always on:
after hours, dispatch, overflow, follow up. One word, plain English, unmistakable on
the phone, no spelling required.

It also survives the vision. An operating partner that is always standing by is a
bigger idea than a receptionist, and the word does not shrink to the phone.

Risk: common word, so the bare .com is almost certainly gone and the trademark space
will have neighbours. Workable with a prefix or a modern extension, but it needs a
real search before anyone commits.

### 2. Second Shift

    Jenny works second shift.

The best emotional fit of anything on this list, because it names the exact hours the
owner cannot cover. Every owner who has taken a call at 7pm understands it instantly
and it requires no explanation at all.

Two words is the cost, and two word names get compressed badly in URLs and logos.
There is also at least one workforce company using it, so the search matters.

### 3. Roster

    Your roster just got bigger.

Staff framing, which is the honest description of what we sell: people who show up.
Plain, one word, spellable, and it hosts a family better than almost anything else
here, because a roster is by definition more than one.

Slightly sporting in flavour, which is either warm or off depending on the room.

### 4. Groundwork

Infrastructure feel. The work underneath the business that has to happen before
anything else does. Expands to anything operational and never boxes into the phone.

Plainer and more serious than the others, which suits a company that wants to be
signed with. Less emotional pull, and it is a fairly used word.

### 5. Crewline

Trade native. Crew is a word these owners use every day about their own people, and
line carries the phone without being trapped by it.

The compound reads a little coined, which cuts against "basic," and the phone
association is stronger than the others, so it may age badly as the product widens.

### 6. Frontline

Strong and obvious, and the phone genuinely is the front line. I am listing it to
recommend against it. The trademark space is crowded with large incumbents in
adjacent categories, and fighting for a name is a bad use of a small company's money.

---

## WHAT I WOULD NOT DO

**No AI in the name.** It dates immediately, it invites the objection before the
conversation starts, and in three years it will read like putting "dot com" in a name
in 2001. The product can be AI. The company should not announce it.

**No coined or misspelled word.** Lee said basic and he is right for this audience.
Contractors are not charmed by a missing vowel, and every misspelled name costs its
owner a lifetime of spelling it over the phone.

**No word that names the current function.** Dispatch, Answer, Reception, Desk. Each
is a good description of today and a ceiling on tomorrow, which is the exact problem
he is trying to escape by moving off "Jenny."

**Not under Junk Doctors**, per Lee, and I would add: keep it off the junk removal
vocabulary entirely. Junk removal is the beachhead, not the market. A name that says
junk cannot follow the product into HVAC.

## DOMAIN AND TRADEMARK, HONESTLY

I have not verified any of these and I am not qualified to clear a trademark. Every
one of the above needs a real domain search and a proper knockout search before it
gets used anywhere, and the trademark question is a lawyer's, not mine.

What I can say is the pattern: single common English words lost their bare .com years
ago, so expect to buy a prefixed or extended variant, and expect the bare .com to cost
five figures if it is for sale at all. That is normal and it is not a reason to reject
a good name. It is a reason to check before falling in love.

# SEARCH RESULTS 2026-08-26

Ran DNS registration checks on 46 candidate domains and web searches on the leading
names. **I ran searches. I did not clear anything. A knockout search by a lawyer is
still required before any name gets used.**

## Domains: the plain word strategy hits a wall

Of 46 domains checked across all five candidates and their common variants, **two were
unregistered**: standbylabs.com and standbyagents.com. Everything else is taken,
including every bare .com, every get/try/join/use prefix, and .ai, .io, .co, .team and
.work for standby.

That is not a fact about our candidates, it is a fact about 2026. Any plain English
word anyone would want has been registered for years.

**Registered does not mean unavailable.** A large share of these are parked and for
sale, so for most of them the real question is price, not availability. standby.com is
registered but does not serve a website at all, which matters: it is not an active
company with a brand, it is somebody holding an asset.

**And we do not need the exact match .com to decide.** The company name is not
customer facing yet. It matters for partnerships, the affiliate program, contracts,
and future products. So the sequence is: pick the right name, use a compound domain to
start, and negotiate for the short one later if it ever matters. Choosing a worse name
because a better one costs money would be the tail wagging the dog.

## Trademark: one real neighbour on Standby

**STANDBY VIRTUAL DESKTOP** is a registered SaaS mark in **International Class 042**,
owned by TetherView LLC, filed 2018, covering software as a service for archiving,
server virtualization, and business continuity.

Class 042 is exactly where we would file. The mark is not identical, it is two words
with a descriptive tail in a different corner of software, and their goods are nothing
like ours. But it is close enough that a bare STANDBY application in 042 would face a
likelihood of confusion question, and that is a lawyer's call, not mine.

Practical read: this does not kill Standby, and it does mean Standby is the candidate
most in need of a real knockout search before anyone spends money on it.

## No competitor collision

No company called Standby appears in home services or AI voice agents. The name is
clear in our actual market, which is the collision that would have been disqualifying.

## Worth knowing, unrelated to naming

The same search turned up **Bravi**, building "an AI operating system for home services
businesses," using AI customer communication as the wedge to replace the front office.
That is close to Lee's stated long term vision, described in nearly his words.

Not a reason to change anything. It is a reason to know they exist, and it is mild
confirmation that the direction is right, since somebody funded is walking the same
road. Worth a proper competitive look at some point, separate from naming.

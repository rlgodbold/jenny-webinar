# The compounding missed revenue math

Built by marketing, 2026-08-27, after Lee greenlit the placement argument. Homes, in
priority order: the tape email, the /watch class, the sales call, a retargeting ad.

---

## THE CENTRAL DESIGN DECISION, AND WHY IT MATTERS LEGALLY AND COMMERCIALLY

**Make the compounding argument qualitatively. Let the owner quantify it himself.**

The deck's version projects a dollar figure: one customer is worth about $2,000 over
about ten years, so one missed bookable call a week is $104,000 given away this year.
That lands hard in a live room where Lee can caveat it conversationally.

Written down and sent to a stranger, that same figure is an **earnings claim**. It
needs a defensible basis, a typicality frame, and under RBG's own placement rule a
disclosure sitting with the number at comparable weight. Which is a lot of apparatus
bolted to our most persuasive sentence.

There is a better version that is both safer and, I think, more persuasive:

    Anchor on the number we actually MEASURED.
    Make the compounding point as a story, not a projection.
    Ask the owner to apply his own average job and his own missed calls.

**A number an owner works out himself is worth more than one we hand him**, because he
believes his own arithmetic and he cannot argue with it. And we are not making an
earnings claim if we are not claiming earnings.

## WHAT IS MEASURED VERSUS WHAT IS MODELLED

RBG will ask this first, so here it is separated cleanly.

### Measured. Real data, defensible.

Source: CallRail missed call report, 4 May to 2 June 2026, in
`~/Documents/LG/CallRail Missed Call Revenue - Last 30 Days/`.

    59 junk removal companies
    719 missed inbound calls across them in 30 days
    ~12 missed calls per company per month
    CallRail unanswered total 730, reconciliation gap of 11

That is a real count of real missed calls at real junk removal companies. **It is the
strongest number we own and we barely use it.**

### Modelled. Assumptions, must be labelled as such.

    0.44   booking rate, applied uniformly to every company in the workbook
    $500   average job size, applied uniformly
    $2,000 customer lifetime revenue over about 10 years
    $158,180 total "estimated missed revenue" — this is 719 x 0.44 x $500, so it
             inherits both assumptions and is NOT a measurement

**Flag for RBG and for Lee:** the deck says "$2,000 in lifetime revenue, over about 10
years, **before referrals and repeat jobs**." That is internally inconsistent. A ten
year lifetime value already contains the repeat jobs. Either the $2,000 includes repeat
work and the phrase should be "before referrals," or it means something else and needs
restating. Worth fixing before it appears anywhere written.

## 1. THE TAPE EMAIL. PRIMARY HOME.

Sent after the prospect hears Jenny answer as their own company. Highest intent moment
in the funnel, and the right place for a rational justification because it arrives
after the emotional yes rather than instead of it.

    SUBJECT   What that call is actually worth

    Hey {FirstName},

    Here is your recording.

    [ Listen to your call ]

    While it is fresh, one number worth sitting with.

    We pulled the missed call reports on 59 junk removal companies for a month. Between
    them they missed 719 calls. That is about a dozen per company, in thirty days, and
    it is counted rather than guessed.

    Here is the part most owners get wrong.

    A missed call is not a lost job. It is a lost customer.

    The person who could not reach you this morning needed a garage cleared. In two
    years they are moving. Four years after that a parent's house needs emptying. They
    would have called you every one of those times, because people call the company
    that answered.

    Instead they called the next name on the list, and that company gets all of it.

    So use your own numbers. Pull last month's missed calls. Multiply by your average
    job. Then ask what that customer was worth over ten years, not over one afternoon.

    That is the leak. She is the patch.

    [ Talk to Lee ]

    Lee Godbold
    Junk Removal Authority

**Why this version.** No projected dollar figure, so no earnings claim and no
disclosure block. The only number is one we counted. The compounding argument arrives
as a story about one person, which is how people actually feel it, and the arithmetic
is homework rather than assertion.

## 2. THE /watch CLASS SECTION

The class is where the full quantified version belongs. Forty minutes of committed
attention, a teaching posture, and a disclosure reads as rigour rather than as
hedging.

Keep the deck's interactive structure, which is already the right shape:

    Calls missed per week   x   % that would book   x   your average job   =  your leak

**Changes I would make to the deck version before it goes on a page:**

- Lead with the 59 companies and 719 calls. Measured first, modelled second.
- Label 0.44 and $500 as the defaults they are, and make them editable.
- Drop the $104,000 headline as a stated figure. Let the calculator produce a number
  from the owner's own inputs instead. Same punch, no claim.
- Keep the speaker note's discipline visible: **revenue, not profit.** That distinction
  is doing real legal work and it should survive into print.

Disclosure, placed above the calculator at comparable weight per RBG's rule:

    Missed call counts are from CallRail reports across 59 junk removal companies,
    May 2026. Booking rate and job value are your inputs. Results vary and this is an
    illustration, not a projection of what your business will earn.

## 3. SALES CALL MATERIAL

Lee's strongest use, and no design constraint at all because he can caveat live.

The sequence that works:

1. Ask how many calls they missed last month. Make them say the number.
2. Ask their average job. Make them say that too.
3. Do the multiplication out loud, slowly, once.
4. Then the turn: **"that is the job. Now think about the customer."**
5. The ten year story, in their words about their own market.
6. Silence.

The deck note is right that the number should sit in silence for a beat. The mistake is
filling it.

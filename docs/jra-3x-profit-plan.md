# Tripling JRA's profit in 12 months

Independent take, marketing and client growth seat. JRA the agency only.
Target: roughly $350k profit today to roughly $1M. So **+$650k of profit**, not revenue.

---

## START WITH THE ARITHMETIC, BECAUSE IT ELIMINATES MOST OF THE OBVIOUS ANSWERS

The instinct is "get more clients." The arithmetic says that is the most expensive route
available, and probably not achievable in twelve months.

**Service revenue and software revenue buy profit at completely different prices.**

    SEO, websites, paid ads     labour delivered, call it 30 to 40% margin
    Jenny                       software, call it 70% contribution after voice and support

To find +$650k of profit:

    via services at 35%     needs about +$1.85M of revenue
                            which needs roughly triple the delivery headcount
                            twelve months is not enough, and margin does not improve with scale

    via Jenny at 70%        needs about +$930k of revenue
                            = about $77k MRR
                            = **roughly 100 Jenny clients at $750**

**That is the whole plan in one number. About 100 Jenny clients triples JRA's profit almost
on its own.** Everything below is either a way to get those 100, a way to need fewer of them,
or a way to keep the ones you get.

It also reframes the assignment. This is not "grow an agency by 3x." It is **"sell a software
product into an audience JRA already owns, and let the agency fund it."** Those are different
companies and only one of them can triple in a year.

Today there are 5 tenants. 100 is a twentyfold increase and I am not going to pretend that is
comfortable. But it is a defined, countable target against a national market of thousands of
junk removal companies, which is a far better problem than "sell more SEO."

## THE LEVERS, RANKED BY PROFIT PER UNIT OF EFFORT

### 1. PRICE. Fastest money in the business, and the one everybody skips.

**Impact: potentially $100k+ of profit from a decision, not a project.**

A price rise drops almost entirely to the bottom line, because delivery cost does not move.
If JRA's revenue is around $1M, **a 10% increase is roughly $100k of profit, which is 15% of
the target, available this quarter with no new clients and no new headcount.**

Agencies chronically underprice, and JRA has unusually strong permission to charge more: the
positioning is "avoid over $100k of mistakes," the founder runs an actual junk removal
company, and no competitor can say both of those things.

**Requires:** a price review per line, a grandfathering decision for existing clients, and the
nerve to lose a few price-sensitive accounts. Losing the bottom 5% on price usually improves
profit twice over, because those accounts consume support disproportionately.

### 2. SELL JENNY TO THE EXISTING CLIENT BASE FIRST.

**Impact: the cheapest Jenny clients JRA will ever get, and they set the case studies.**

Every existing JRA client is a junk removal owner with a phone, an existing relationship,
existing trust, and existing billing details. **Customer acquisition cost is close to zero.**
No ad spend, no cold outreach, no trust to build.

If JRA has meaningful client numbers, converting a third of them is a realistic quarter of
work and it moves a serious fraction of the 100.

**Requires:** the client count, which I do not have. This is the single number that most
changes the plan.

**And a warning that matters more than it sounds:** these must be sold, not bundled in
silently. A client who feels cross-sold gets less trusting of everything else JRA sells. The
right frame is diagnostic, which is what the CallRail data already enables: show them their
own answer rate, then offer the fix.

### 3. RETENTION. In a recurring business this is a profit lever disguised as an ops problem.

**Impact: comparable to acquisition, at a fraction of the cost.**

Halving churn roughly doubles average client lifetime, which compounds into profit without
selling anything. In a business where profit is recurring, **the churn rate is worth more than
the close rate**, and it is almost always cheaper to move.

It also compounds with lever 2: Jenny clients who churn at 3% a month need constant
replacement to hold 100; at 1.5% the same sales effort accumulates instead of treading water.

**Requires:** JRA's actual churn by line, which I do not have. If churn is already low, this
lever drops down the list. If it is not, it may outrank everything above it.

### 4. PRODUCTISE THE SERVICES so revenue can grow without headcount.

**Impact: raises margin on existing revenue and removes the delivery ceiling.**

Custom scope is what caps an agency's margin. Fixed packages with defined deliverables raise
margin on the same revenue by killing scope creep, and they make delivery trainable rather
than dependent on the best people.

This is what makes lever 5 survivable. Without it, winning more service clients just buys more
work.

**Requires:** a scoping exercise per service line and the discipline to say no to bespoke.

### 5. NEW CLIENT ACQUISITION. Real, but the most expensive dollar of profit here.

**Impact: necessary for scale, worst ratio of the five.**

Everyone reaches for this first. It is last on this list because it costs media, time and
delivery capacity, and because the four levers above produce profit from clients JRA already
has.

The exception is Jenny specifically, which does not consume delivery capacity the way service
lines do. **Acquisition for Jenny is a good lever; acquisition for services is a trap until
lever 4 is done.**

**The instrument already exists:** the CallRail data ranks 62 real companies by a measurable
weakness, and the addressable-share finding says the worst leakers are 79% fixable. That is
the sharpest prospecting asset in the business and it is currently gated on legal.

## POSITIONING AGAINST AVOCA, GHI AND THE REST

**Do not compete as an AI company. JRA will lose that fight and it is the wrong fight.**

The AI voice agent category is about to commoditise. Well funded horizontal players serve
HVAC, plumbing, electrical and junk removal from one platform, and their model prices are the
same models we use. **Feature parity is temporary and feature superiority is not defensible.**

JRA's actual advantage is structural and cannot be copied quickly:

**Nobody else can run the business.** Avoca sells software. JRA sells the operating system for
a junk removal company: what to charge, which truck to buy, how to hire, how to rank, and now
who answers the phone. A generalist AI vendor cannot tell an owner what a hot tub removal
should cost in his market. **That is domain knowledge accumulated over a decade of actually
operating, and it is the moat.**

**The bundle is the defensibility.** Jenny wired into the website JRA built, quoting from the
price list JRA set, booking into the CRM JRA configured, fed by the ads JRA runs, is very hard
to rip out. A standalone AI receptionist is a monthly line item anyone can cancel. **Every
additional integration point raises switching cost**, which is worth more than any feature.

**The positioning line:** not "the AI receptionist for junk removal" but **"the people who
actually run junk removal companies, and now they answer your phone too."** The proof is that
Lee runs one.

**One risk to name:** that advantage is currently embodied in Lee personally. That is a
strength in marketing and a weakness in valuation, which is the next section.

## THE VALUATION HALF, AND IT IS THE SAME ACTION

He asked for profit AND valuation. They point the same way, which is unusually convenient.

**Mix matters more than magnitude.** Agency services trade at roughly 3 to 5x earnings.
Recurring software trades far higher, because the revenue is predictable and does not require
proportional labour. **A business at $1M profit that is majority recurring software is worth
materially more than one at $1M of agency profit**, and it is the same $1M.

So the mix shift toward Jenny does two jobs at once: it is the cheapest route to the profit
target AND it re-rates the multiple. Nothing else on this list does both.

**Two things that discount the multiple regardless of profit, and both are fixable:**

**Owner dependence.** Right now Lee is the brand, the sales function, and the credibility.
A buyer discounts heavily for that. Building a sales motion that closes without him being on
the call is a valuation project as much as a capacity one.

**Revenue concentration and contract length.** Month to month recurring is worth less than
annual. Even a modest shift to annual terms with a discount improves both cash and multiple.

## TWELVE MONTH SEQUENCE

**Quarter 1: take the free money.**
Price review and increase. Jenny into the existing client base using their own CallRail
numbers as the diagnostic. Fix the measurement so everything after this is decidable: close
rate per line, churn per line, CAC per line. **No new headcount, no new spend.**

**Quarter 2: stop the bucket leaking, then productise.**
Churn work on whichever line is worst. Fixed-scope service packages. Jenny onboarding made
repeatable so client 40 costs less to onboard than client 4. This quarter is what makes Q3
possible.

**Quarter 3 and 4: scale acquisition, Jenny first.**
Paid, the CallRail prospecting engine once legal clears it, and the affiliate program if it
turns out to be worth extending. Services acquisition only after productisation holds.

The order is deliberate: **every quarter funds the next one, and nothing requires capital or
headcount before the profit that pays for it exists.**

## THE NUMBERS THAT WOULD CHANGE THIS PLAN

I have given a structure, not fake precision, because I do not have JRA's internals. Five
numbers would sharpen it considerably, and two of them could reorder the list:

1. **Client count and revenue per line.** Decides how much of the 100 comes from the existing
   base, which is the difference between a comfortable plan and a hard one.
2. **Churn by line.** If churn is high, lever 3 moves to the top and acquisition is pointless
   until it is fixed.
3. **True Jenny unit economics.** My 70% margin is an estimate. If voice costs are materially
   higher, the 100 becomes 130 and the plan gets harder.
4. **Current service margins.** Decides whether productisation is a modest gain or a large one.
5. **Delivery capacity.** How many new service clients JRA could actually onboard before
   quality drops.

**On the arithmetic above:** treat every figure as a structural estimate, not a forecast. The
shape of the argument, that profit tripling comes from mix shift rather than volume, holds
across a wide range of those inputs. The specific number of Jenny clients does not.
